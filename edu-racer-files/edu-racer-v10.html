About this data

Mean PISA mathematics scores for 15-year-olds. 79 of the 81 PISA 2022 sovereign participants are available, plus England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland shown separately alongside the combined UK figure — 83 selectable entries in total. 25 of the 79 countries have the full eight-cycle history back to 2000; the other 54 currently show only their verified 2022 score, appearing as a single point rather than a full trajectory (see "Coverage" below) — historical data for those can be added over time.

Coverage and exclusions

Two of the 81 published PISA 2022 rows are left out entirely: "Baku (Azerbaijan)" and "Ukrainian regions (18 of 27)" are partial/subnational samples, not full-country results — the same reason China (Shanghai-only in 2009/2012, a wider but still partial sample later) is excluded. Singapore has no data before 2009 (it didn't participate yet); its line starts there rather than being drawn back to zero. The United Kingdom's figures are only OECD-comparable from 2006 onward — its 2000 and 2003 rounds both had response-rate problems and are excluded rather than shown as unreliable numbers.

About the flags

Flags are drawn as plain SVG shapes — colour bands, crosses, circles — not emoji or image files, so they render identically on every device rather than depending on a font that may or may not include them. Most are simplified: correct colours and basic layout, but stars, emblems, crests and fine detail are generally omitted. A handful of PISA participants aren't standard sovereign ISO 3166-1 entries (Chinese Taipei, Kosovo, the Palestinian Authority); their codes follow the closest widely-used convention rather than a formal ISO code.

Why "GBR"?

Every code on this chart (FIN, JPN, GBR, and so on) is the ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 code — the same three-letter country codes used by the UN, ISO, and most passport and banking systems worldwide. This is not a choice made by this app: under that international standard, the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland is simply coded GBR, the same way Germany is DEU and Switzerland is CHE. See the separate note below on what the UK's underlying PISA figure actually represents.

A note on "United Kingdom"

The UK figure is the OECD's single combined result for England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland weighted together — not England alone. The four nations diverge meaningfully: in PISA 2022, England's maths score was well above Wales's, with Scotland and Northern Ireland in between. Treat "United Kingdom" here as a UK-wide average, not a stand-in for any one nation.

England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland

Alongside the combined "United Kingdom" figure, the four UK nations are available separately. Their historical depth varies by nation and is shown honestly rather than padded: England has all six comparable cycles back to 2006, Scotland has five (2009 not independently verified here), Wales and Northern Ireland only go back to 2012 in the sources checked for this app. These aren't PISA sovereign participants in their own right — the OECD samples and reports them within the UK's overall participation — so their codes are ISO 3166-2:GB subdivision codes (ENG, SCT, WLS, NIR) rather than ISO 3166-1 country codes. Northern Ireland has no single uncontested national flag, so it's shown with a plain neutral marker rather than a specific flag design.

Sources

OECD Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA). 2000–2018 scores for the 25 full-history countries via World Bank EdStats / DataHub compilation. 2022 scores for all 79 countries via the OECD PISA 2022 Database (Table I.B1.2.1), as compiled by EducationMetrics.org's PISA 2022 ranking table. UK comparability note from the OECD's PISA 2022 country note for the United Kingdom. England/Scotland/Wales/NI figures via Oxford Department of Education's PISA digest (drawing on the official England, Northern Ireland and Wales national reports), with England's 2006/2009 figures from PISA 2022: National Report for England and Scotland's 2006 figure from Scottish Government PISA reporting.
