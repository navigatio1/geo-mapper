About this data

"Life evaluation" scores from the World Happiness Report 2026 (Figure 2.1 data table), each a 3-year rolling average of Gallup World Poll respondents rating their own life on a 0 (worst possible) to 10 (best possible) ladder. 167 countries and territories are selectable, with coverage ranging from a full 2011–2025 run (129 of them) down to just a handful of scattered years for some of the hardest-to-survey places.

Why some lines are short or gappy

Gallup doesn't survey every country every year — coverage is driven entirely by where fieldwork was possible. No year is ever filled in or estimated here: a gap in a line means that year's figure wasn't in the source table, not that it was zero or unchanged. A few countries (Afghanistan, Somalia, Somaliland, North Cyprus among them) have real but sparse coverage, often ending abruptly where fieldwork became impossible.

The missing year: 2013

Almost no country in the source table has a 2013 figure — this is a known gap in the report's own historical series, not an omission made here. Because the chart's time axis is spaced by step, not by calendar year, the 2012→2014 interval looks the same width as every other step but actually spans two calendar years. Worth keeping in mind when reading the slope of any line across that point.

3-year averages, not annual snapshots

Each point smooths together that year's survey with the previous two years' — standard practice in the report to reduce small-sample noise, especially for less-populous countries. This means a sharp real-world shock (a war, a pandemic year) shows up as a more gradual slope here than in a single-year figure, and two adjacent points share two of their three underlying survey years, so they're not fully independent readings.

Eswatini / Swaziland

The country now called Eswatini was named Swaziland in the source table's earlier rows (the official renaming took effect in 2018). Both are merged here into a single "Eswatini" line, since they are the same country under two names, not two countries.

Hong Kong, Taiwan, and other non-UN-member entries

"Hong Kong SAR of China" and "Taiwan Province of China" are shown as the source table names them — Gallup surveys them separately from mainland China, so their figures are kept separate here too. Kosovo, North Cyprus and Somaliland are partially-recognised or unrecognised territories reported as their own rows in the source data; none of these five are passing comment on anyone's preferred political status, only following how Gallup itself collected the underlying survey data.

About the codes and flags

Codes are ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 where one exists. Three entries don't have an official ISO code and use an invented, clearly non-standard three-letter substitute instead: Kosovo (XKX, the same widely-used unofficial code seen in other contexts), North Cyprus (NCY) and Somaliland (SML). Flags are drawn as plain SVG shapes — colour bands, crosses, circles — not emoji or image files, so they render identically on every device. Most are simplified: correct colours and basic layout, but stars, emblems, crests and fine detail are generally omitted. Somaliland and North Cyprus in particular are rendered as simplified colour fields rather than their actual flag designs (both feature a star-and-crescent or star emblem this system can't draw), and Eswatini's flag (traditionally with a shield and spears) is likewise reduced to its three main colour bands.

What's not shown

The source file also breaks each score down into six "explained by" components (GDP per capita, social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom, generosity, perceptions of corruption) plus a residual. Only the overall life-evaluation score is charted here; the breakdown is a natural candidate for a future version.

The map

Each country is coloured by its life-evaluation score for the year you pick (brighter = higher). The colour scale is fixed, so the same colour means the same score in every year; scores at or below 2 share the darkest colour. Grey means that country has no figure for that year in the source table, or is not covered by the report at all (North Korea, for example) — never an estimate. Like the line chart, the year slider steps through the 14 years that have data, so it jumps from 2012 straight to 2014. Very small countries (Singapore, Malta, Bahrain, the Maldives, Hong Kong and others) are also drawn as dots so they stay visible and tappable; zoom in for their outlines. A gold outline marks the countries currently chosen for the line chart, and the details panel can add or remove a country from it.

Outlines are from Natural Earth (public domain), simplified for file size and shown in the Equal Earth projection, which keeps country areas comparable. Where borders are disputed (Kashmir and Western Sahara, for example) the map follows that dataset's drawing and takes no position. Kosovo, Somaliland, Northern Cyprus, Taiwan, Hong Kong and Palestine are drawn as separate areas only because the source table reports them separately.

Sources

World Happiness Report 2026, Chapter 2, Figure 2.1 data table (Gallup World Poll life evaluations, 3-year rolling averages, 2011–2025). Map outlines: Natural Earth via the world-atlas package.
